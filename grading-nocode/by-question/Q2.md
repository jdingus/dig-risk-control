**Expected answer (from the committed key):** cases_2021 = 1599; cases_2022 = 2671; cases_2023 = 2107; cases_2024 = 1897; cases_2025 = 1684; year_with_most = 2022

## How answers are scored

Each question has two or more required numbers. Per number: within tolerance = full, within the wider band
= half, otherwise zero. A question scores 2 (all numbers full), 1 (at least half the credit) or 0.
Percentages: full within ±0.5 points, half within ±2 points. Counts: full within ±1%, half within ±5%.
Dollar amounts: full if exact, half within ±10%. A named answer (a year, a company) must match exactly.
An answer labelled "estimate" is scored the same way: no credit for refusing, no penalty for saying so.

## Answer Q2-01

**Cases per year (order-date year):**

| Year | Cases | Orders |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 1,603 | 11 |
| 2022 | 2,672 | 12 |
| 2023 | 2,107 | 14 |
| 2024 | 1,897 | 11 |
| 2025 | 1,684 | 10 |

Total: 9,963 cases across 58 orders.

**Most cases: 2022**, with 2,672. It is about 565 more than 2023, the next highest.

Basis and caveats:
- Each case was counted by its "Case Number:" line in the extracted text. The per-order counts match the page counts (pages minus the cover page). The "RecommendationForm" header count is 9,965, two more than the case-number count, so the case total could be off by a couple of cases. This does not change the yearly ranking.
- I could not render the PDFs, so I could not read the order dates printed on the orders. I took each order's date from its filename (MMDDYY, or "09.04.24" for one file). The text does not contain the order date.
- Three 2023 orders have very few cases (ord_PSD_080923: 3; ord_PSD_121323: 2; ord_PSD_103123: 13). I counted them as extracted. If the extraction missed cases, the 2023 total could be understated, but it would need about 565 missing cases to overtake 2022.
