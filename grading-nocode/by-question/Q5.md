# Q5

**Question:** Training option: among cases with a headline penalty above $0, what percentage have a recommendation that contains the word "training" (any capitalization)? Round to one decimal.

**Expected answer (from the committed key):** pct_training = 44.7

## How answers are scored

Each question has two or more required numbers. Per number: within tolerance = full, within the wider band
= half, otherwise zero. A question scores 2 (all numbers full), 1 (at least half the credit) or 0.
Percentages: full within ±0.5 points, half within ±2 points. Counts: full within ±1%, half within ±5%.
Dollar amounts: full if exact, half within ±10%. A named answer (a year, a company) must match exactly.
An answer labelled "estimate" is scored the same way: no credit for refusing, no penalty for saying so.

## Answer Q5-01

**Answer: 29.7%** (2,343 of 7,891 cases with a headline penalty above $0 have a recommendation containing "training").

Method: I counted with ripgrep over the 58 text files in `data/text`, matching each recommendation block (from "recommends the following civil penalty:" up to "Abstentions").

- **Cases with a headline penalty above $0:** 7,891. These are recommendations with a "$" amount. No recommendation showed "$0".
- **Of those, containing "training" (any case):** 2,343.
- **Training-only recommendations with no dollar amount:** 50 more cases contain "training" but have a headline penalty of $0, so they are excluded from the denominator. This is why the all-case training count is 2,393.

2,343 / 7,891 = 29.69%, which rounds to 29.7%.

Caveats:
- The text files are OCR-extracted. I checked for spaced-out spellings of "training" and for "Trainings" or "retrain" and found no additional matches.
- "Case Number" appears 9,963 times and "civil penalty:" 9,955 times, so about 8 forms may lack a parsable recommendation block. I did not resolve those individually, so the denominator could be off by a few cases.
- Bash was unavailable in this session, so the counts come from Grep match counts rather than a script that parsed each form individually.
