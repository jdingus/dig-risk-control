**Expected answer (from the committed key):** cases_2021 = 1599; cases_2022 = 2671; cases_2023 = 2107; cases_2024 = 1897; cases_2025 = 1684; year_with_most = 2022

## How answers are scored

Each question has two or more required numbers. Per number: within tolerance = full, within the wider band
= half, otherwise zero. A question scores 2 (all numbers full), 1 (at least half the credit) or 0.
Percentages: full within ±0.5 points, half within ±2 points. Counts: full within ±1%, half within ±5%.
Dollar amounts: full if exact, half within ±10%. A named answer (a year, a company) must match exactly.
An answer labelled "estimate" is scored the same way: no credit for refusing, no penalty for saying so.

## Answer Q2-01

**2022 has the most cases, with 2,672.**

| Year | Cases |
|---|---|
| 2021 | 1,603 |
| 2022 | 2,672 |
| 2023 | 2,107 |
| 2024 | 1,897 |
| 2025 | 1,684 |
| **Total** | **9,963** |

I counted one case per recommendation-form page after each order's cover page, and took the year from the order date in the file name. For three orders (the 2021-04-21 and 2021-11-03 orders, and the 2022 nunc pro tunc order), I counted pages instead of text matches. The scan text for those orders garbled some "finds the following violation(s)" lines, so a text match would have missed a few cases.

The 2022 total includes the 2022-04-06 nunc pro tunc order. It re-issues one form, for case 24633, to correct a street address. Case 24633 was already approved in the 2022-02-23 order, so it appears twice in the 2022 count. If you treat that correction as a duplicate rather than a case, 2022 drops to 2,671. The ranking doesn't change.

## Answer Q2-02

Cases by order year (one case is one recommendation form, so I counted rows). The total is 9,958 cases from 58 orders.

| Year | Cases | Share of 9,958 |
|---|---|---|
| 2021 | 1,599 | 16.06% |
| 2022 | 2,671 | 26.82% |
| 2023 | 2,107 | 21.16% |
| 2024 | 1,897 | 19.05% |
| 2025 | 1,684 | 16.91% |

**2022 has the most cases, at 2,671.**

Command, run from the folder root: `python3 reference/tools/cases.py group --by year --sort key --receipts 1`

Example case from the top year (2022): order file `ord_PSDNPT_040622.pdf`, page 2, case 24633.

These are cases approved in each order year, not damages that happened that year. Orders are dated unevenly, so a change from one year to the next does not show a change in dig damage.
